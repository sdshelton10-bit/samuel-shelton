Untitled

Fiction/Short Story

James finds himself sitting alone on the front porch of his four bedroom, three bath house in the suburb he always imagined he and his wife living in. His wife and two kids were passed out for a few hours now, leaving himself in silence for this period of time. He has had this itch in the back of his head since he could ever remember, and he now wonders what could be the root of this thing bugging him after all this time.

James grew up in a similar suburb that he currently resides in. James's dream job was to fight forest fires, as he had a passion for the greater good. As a toddler, he always found himself playing with fire trucks and pretending to put out fires with the big imagination all little kids have. Time went on and his reasoning for wanting to be a firefighter changed. He now dreamed that one day his doings would all add up to saving the world, in a sense, and instead of just pretending he wanted to make this a reality. There was just something of that regard that struck his interest, and he always had this image of himself that one day he would achieve his goal. He thought to himself that maybe he wanted to do this because he had had it so easy in his formative years, and it was his duty to pay it forward through a job as risky, but also as salient as extinguishing those colossal flames.

Growing up James had many friends, and accrued many more as time progressed through middle and high school. He never sought out that “popular” title, but this came naturally as he was very personable and an easy conversationalist. He played football and baseball, fairly good at both, which inevitably contributed to his likeness. He eventually graduated grade school and encountered the decisions of where to attend college. He looked back on his secondary school experiences and in the back of his mind he missed the version of himself that wanted to be a firefighter. He missed who he saw himself as. Not so much because he didn’t feel the same feelings about firefighting as he once did. And not so much because he was necessarily ashamed of what he was doing now. James missed the ambition that once was; he felt as if he missed out on an opportunity by not striving for it. He realized that he missed out on what could have been. Although he felt as if he had no regrets, it was in his subconscious where he held the most guilt. But, looking back at it all, “How much could I really change?” he thought to himself.

He chose the top college of choice and went on to study for an engineering degree, just as his parents and friends thought he would. He thought about his past aspirations less and less while simultaneously picking up new hobbies in his first year in college. He was never the party type, though he did find himself drinking with his close circle of friends, some new, some old, every now and then in between weeks. The days were long and the years were short. By the time James entered his Junior year, after all the hard classes - which were hard for most, and a cake walk for James - he found himself with a girlfriend. He had only had about two or three girlfriends prior to this, but none of them compared. The women of the past were mostly flings or partners he liked to have around but would never go so far to marry. This new girl, on the other hand, was different. She was a smart girl who knew what she wanted, and what she wanted James had. They dated until they both graduated and eventually the summer after, James asked her to marry him, to which she said, “I do!”

James has had a hard time with girls in his past. He has never had a hard time getting them, the hard part was getting himself to like them. So many beautiful girls throw themselves at James, and he blows them off because of their lack of personalities. This new girl, however, caught James attention not due to her looks, but due to her personality. He has had this image of the perfect girl in his head since he can remember, and this woman checks off a bunch of the boxes. Eventually James told himself that he imagined their littles creations running around, and that’s how he knew she was the “one.” She wasn’t the perfect girl, nor did he try to shop around for any while longer, but he felt a way that he never had before. And, of course, he didn’t believe in perfection.

His wife got all her wedding parties and little decorations perfect for the perfect day, and it was just that. James had a bachelor’s party, nothing crazy. It wasn’t long after they had gotten married that they had kids and then eventually another one. Their life, on paper, was perfect. Of course they got into silly arguments and were raising two kids which makes things inevitably harder. But, it was good. There was no denying in James’s head that this life was good. He now had a higher up position at his engineering firm, a loving wife, and two beautiful kids. All of this, however, brought James back to this night, on the front porch of the ranch style home that he built. Not often does James think about the negative things in life. Nor does he contemplate what he could have done, especially on a day to day basis. This was different. This was life altering decisions that led him here.

In high school, James would read. Not a whole lot, but enough to keep him sharp where he felt was important. He once read this book that was given to him by his grandpa. His grandpa was an awesome man. Almost a typical grandparent, had fought in the war, had a stable job, and an adorable family with many great achievements. James really looked up to his grandpa, and he respected him immensely because he had raised his dad, who was also a family man, and overall was the head of a family that he loved. However, there was something that stood out about him. He told stories that would leave James in awe for hours. There was this one that James really loved. It went along the lines of how he had just moved to a new city. He knew no one, but was so excited it didn’t bother him. He started a job at a newspaper, as he wanted to eventually become the one writing. He ran errands and practiced in his free time. He would essentially just write for the newspaper but it wasn’t his writing getting published. His grandpa praised this job like it was valhalla. The pay was not good, and this amazing opportunity soon withered as he was let go about eight months later. His grandpa told this story to James a couple times, and it always stuck with him the most.

On his second glass of whiskey James began to check the time. He had been sitting outside for about an hour now, just reminiscing about his past, and a little bit about his future. He thought back on his college days and one instance came up that he had not thought about in a long time. It was probably his sophomore year when he had an idea that he was spit balling when he was drinking one night with his group of friends. A lot of nights they would do this and wake up the next morning and know it was all just bullshit. One night, though, things were a little off in this conversation. Not in any particularly bad way, but just off.

It was around ten o’clock when everybody was on or around their eighth beer, in between that heavy buzz and drunk state. This is where all the bright ideas come to mind. So, as the night is going they decide to stay in, rightfully so as it is a Wednesday. One person who James doesn’t usually hangout with, or not quite as often, brings up an interesting, yet borderline stupid topic about how everyone who has ever dropped out has gotten rich, or has gotten everything they wanted such as fame and riches. To name a couple he mentioned Bill Gates, Mark Zuckerberg, and a few more that he remembers were notable, but not coming to mind. Actors and such. This friend of his goes on to say that he wants to drop out and be like these people. Everyone starts to laugh at him, including James, and then he laughs at himself knowing that this won’t actually happen, but in a perfect world would seem plausible. He said he wanted to drop out and start a business and that he would be a success, as everyone cheered and continued on with their night full of fun.

James doesn’t necessarily miss college, but he understood that he would never be that young and free again. He misses the nights with all of his friends, going to class and talking to all of the smart girls, walking around campus. He even missed the bad parts of it. Failing exams, rejection. He understood that this is a part of life and you must take the good with the bad. Thanks to a couple of philosophy general studies, he learned a bit that helped him out in reasoning why life is difficult and what to do to move past it. Of course, college is a bubble and things turn real after one graduates. It is easy to get caught up in the small, trivial scenarios that college puts you through, but it also sets you up for the real world, as one might say. This was demonstrated in James’s life a couple years after graduation, when he was in the workforce. There was an employee who he wasn’t close with, but would work closely with that had passed away tragically in a drunk driving incident. The person driving was arrested, but must live with that guilt for the rest of their life, which to James, was much worse than prison.

This coworker of James was more of a quiet type. He worked closely with James and they had talked about blueprints and such together, but not far too much beyond that and just making small talk at company parties. James knew him enough to know that he is alive and has a soul. So hearing this devastating news shook up James. This was now ten something years ago, at the beginning of his career, but now as he looks back the same feelings come with it as if it had just happened. It’s very easy to let the simple parts of life get away from you. It’s also very easy to take what you have for granted. James would wonder about this coworker, all of the dreams, all of the family and friends, stripped away in the blink of an eye. Not thinking much of anything. Just thinking about how his life is moving day to day. It’s very cliche. The saying to live everyday to the fullest. This is mostly because it’s not very possible. You can’t just spend everyday in Bali. Unless you are up for leaving your entire life behind. And at the point what is it even for. These ideas penetrate James’s consciousness pretty frequently. He doesn’t classify himself as pessimistic, though, it may come off as that. But, James has been more so a realist. After the death of his coworker, he made extra efforts to show the ones he cared about his appreciativeness towards them. Although, after a while these motions faded away and everything shifted back as if the tragic accident had never happened. Not on purpose, but because that is how life works, aware or unaware of it.

As James’s thoughts on this topic conclude, new thoughts about his first born child arise. He remembers this time, when his kid was around five or six, made a comment about wanting to be a firefighter. His child was super excited when his kindergarten class took a field trip to the local fire department. His child’s eyes glowed as he sat in the front seat of the fire truck, but he was sure the pictures did not do him justice of the excitement that his son felt. For weeks after this his son would rave about fire trucks and firemen. He would always yell with excitement when seeing one, and went on to call them superheroes as they sped by. It was up until around middle school that he stopped mentioning the once glorified profession. Not because he stopped loving them, but because there had been too much going on in his rapidly expanding life. This made James feel a certain way. Not sad. Not mad. But disappointed. Not in his son but in himself. James felt as if it was his responsibility to keep his son’s fire alive for what he felt was true. Something that he wished someone had done for him.

James now thought about his life as a whole. He thought about all the failed relationships, all the jobs he liked, didn’t like. All the mistakes and all the experiences that brought him happiness. He felt a pit, though. Something was missing and he didn’t know what. He had become a successful engineer, married a good girl. Made his parents proud. One thing was missing though. Had he made himself proud? He started thinking about deep down what he really wanted. He really wanted what he wished he pushed his son for. Being a firefighter. He thought about his past life. He thought to himself that maybe he could have done better, been better. His now wife, who he grew to love, was not who he had wanted to marry, but someone who had worked. He did still love her and wouldn’t trade her, but it had opened his mind up to thoughts about settling throughout his life, and not taking the chances that deep down he wanted to take. What would have happened if he took after his grandpa and followed a little dream, even if it wasn’t what his parents had wanted? What if he took a route that wasn’t all the way thought out, and had no sign of success, but had all the signs of what he really, truly wanted to do? James was now questioning everything he has ever done. He starts to think now that maybe he has messed up his entire life. He comes back to reality and accepts how his life has turned out. He realizes there will always be a what if, and it is much easier to say that life would have been better if you did this that or the other. But, he also knows that life could have been much grander if said things were to work out in his favor and he followed his “dreams.”

He now questioned the word ‘love.’ Has he really done anything that brought him such fire to his heart that he would give up the entire world for? He thinks not, but he starts to think that there could have been. Being the level head that he is, he knows he can't go into the past and change anything, he can only change the future, but just about everything in his life has been set in stone. James is now done outside as he finishes his second glass of whiskey. He crawls into bed with his wife who he loves, after he kisses his two beautiful kids goodnight. His wife rolls over to grab his face and mention that she is glad that he finally came to bed. James closes his eyes and falls asleep rather quickly, failing to realize he has loved his entire life but has never been in love for his entire life.
